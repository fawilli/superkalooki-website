Kalooki is a variant form of Contract Rummy, played by 4–6 players using multiple decks of 54 standard playing cards (52 + 2 jokers). The objective is to have the lowest cumulative score, or to go out by laying down all of your cards.

Threes & Fours

What is a Three?

A three is a set of three or more cards of the same rank. Suits do not matter; duplicates are allowed. Multiple threes from one player must be of different ranks. A minimum three must contain at least two genuine (non-joker) cards.

What is a Four?

A four is a run of four or more consecutive cards of the same suit. Aces may be high or low but only at the end of a run. Multiple fours from one player must be of different suits. Jokers cannot occupy two consecutive positions in a four.

Joker Rules

A joker placed in a three or four can never be removed. A joker in a three cannot move at all. A joker in a four may be moved to the end of the run by the holder of the natural card — but never to a different meld.

Gameplay

A Player’s Turn

  1. Draw one card from the draw pile or top of the discard pile.
  2. Optionally lay down all cards. Going out on the same turn you first lay is “down and out” — other players score double.
  3. Discard any one card (not a joker) face-up.

Calling

If you have not yet laid down, you may call a card just discarded by another player. The active player may allow or deny the call. If allowed: caller takes the discard plus draws one extra penalty card; they cannot lay or discard on that call-turn. Maximum 3 calls per player per hand. Players who have already laid down cannot call.

Laying Down

Your first lay must fully satisfy the contract for that deal. If you cannot meet the contract you cannot lay any cards.

Scoring & Winning

When a player goes out, all others add remaining card values to their cumulative score. After all nine deals, the player with the lowest cumulative score wins.

Penalties

Over-Call Penalty

More than three calls in one round earns a 50-point penalty. The player may no longer call, refuse calls, or go “down and out” for the rest of that game.

Contract Error Penalty

Laying cards that do not satisfy the required contract earns a 50-point penalty. The player cannot lay or go “down and out” that round — drawing and discarding only.
